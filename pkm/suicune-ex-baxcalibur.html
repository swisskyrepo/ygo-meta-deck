General Strategy

What the deck wants to do every game

The deck is an energy-acceleration tempo build. You rush the Frigibax → Baxcalibur line online (often via Rare Candy), then use Baxcalibur's Ice Maker ability to attach an extra Water Energy from your Energy Zone every turn — on top of your natural turn energy. That doubled energy flow lets your attackers hit a turn ahead of the curve.

Your payoff is Suicune ex and its Crystal Waltz attack, which deals 20 damage for every Benched Pokémon in play (yours and the opponent's). With your own bench full of the Baxcalibur line plus Chien-Pao, and the opponent developing their board, Crystal Waltz quickly climbs past 100+ damage. Chien-Pao ex is the secondary heavy-hitter / sniping closer, and Cyrus drags a wounded benched threat into the Active spot so you can finish it for free tempo.

The game plan: survive the early turns, stabilize the energy engine, then convert your board advantage into back-to-back knockouts before the opponent's Mega ex attackers can out-muscle your 140-HP line.

Pros & Cons

Where the deck shines and where it folds

✓ Strengths

  • Repeatable energy acceleration — Baxcalibur attaches an extra Water Energy every turn, letting you out-tempo slower setups.
  • Scaling damage — Crystal Waltz snowballs with a full board; punishes wide benches hard.
  • Two strong ex attackers — Suicune ex and Chien-Pao ex give you redundant win conditions.
  • Surgical knockouts — Cyrus + a hard hit picks off the opponent's key benched Pokémon.
  • Proven results — consistent Tier-2 placement (Power 35.0) with multiple community-tournament toppings.

✗ Weaknesses

  • Stage-2 reliance — bricks if Baxcalibur or Rare Candy never show up; clunky opening hands.
  • Board-dependent damage — Crystal Waltz is weak into lone-attacker / empty-bench decks.
  • Fragile 140-HP bodies — Mega ex hitters out-size your attackers; Water is weak to Lightning.
  • Rough vs the #1 deck — loses to Miraidon ex / Magnezone (~36%) and Mega Lucario ex / Greninja (~33%).
  • Consistency tax — needs its draw supporters (Professor's Research / Copycat) to keep the engine fed.

Key Combos & Lines

The sequences that win games

1
Frigibax → Rare Candy → Baxcalibur

Skip the Arctibax middle stage and bring the energy engine online a full turn early. This is your priority play turns 2–3.

2
Baxcalibur "Ice Maker" → load Suicune ex / Chien-Pao ex

Every turn, attach an extra Water Energy from the Energy Zone onto an attacker — stacking energy faster than the opponent can keep pace.

3
Full bench → Suicune ex "Crystal Waltz"

20 damage × every Benched Pokémon (both sides). Keep your own Baxcalibur line + Chien-Pao benched, and the opponent's development only feeds your damage.

4
Cyrus → drag wounded Bench Pokémon → KO

Pull a damaged or key benched threat into the Active spot, then knock it out with Suicune or Chien-Pao for a free tempo swing.

5
Giant Cape on Suicune ex + Pokémon Center Lady

Push your attacker to 160 HP and heal 30 between turns to keep your scaling attacker on the board through Mega ex punches.

6
Professor's Research / Copycat → refuel

Dump and redraw to keep finding Rare Candy, Poké Ball, and your second attacker. Copycat is huge when the opponent has a fat hand.

Variants & Alternatives

Common swaps and sibling builds

Disruption package

Swap a flex slot for Mars — force the opponent to shuffle their hand and draw fewer cards, slowing combo decks that hoard resources. Pairs well against the grindy Miraidon matchup.

Search consistency

Run Juliana to dig for your Basics (Frigibax / Suicune ex) more reliably, smoothing the clunky openings that plague Stage-2 lines.

Tech vs Tools & Stadiums

Field Blower is a flex/tech slot — strip the opponent's Giant Cape, defensive tools, or a problematic Stadium. Cut it in matchups where it's dead.

Sibling archetypes

The Baxcalibur Water engine also powers Suicune ex / Greninja / Giratina ex bench-damage shells and pure Chien-Pao ex snipe builds. Same engine, different payoff attacker.
